Ops ​

The event vocabulary appended to refs/sturn/wal/<ws>. JSON, content-addressed (blob sha = event id), one commit per append.

Tree ops ​

OpFieldsEffect at snap
writepath, blob, intent?replace/create file
renamefrom, tomove entry
deletepathremove entry

Multi-workspace merge ​

sturn snap a b … folds several workspaces' pending ops into one commit — rename-aware, not a bare path concat:

  • a write onto a path another ws renamed away follows the rename to its destination (the modify-vs-rename case a 3-way merge resolves the same way), chained f→g→h and applied after the rename no matter the ws order;
  • genuinely ambiguous shapes stay put and warn on stderr instead of picking a silent winner: a source renamed two ways (rename f diverged), a delete meeting a rename (either side), colliding destinations, and cross-ws same-path write/delete collisions.

Claims ​

claim.glob carries a resource URI — scheme:path. fs: globs (src/**, and a bare glob means fs:) or opaque resources (dev:display0, sess:T-012, cap:role). net: paths may be port ranges — net:13000-13100 conflicts with net:13050; non-numeric paths fall back to prefix matching.

Each live claim derives a fencing token at fold time — a Lamport-style epoch: 1 + max over earlier claims on overlapping URIs, ordered by ts then WAL position. Never stored, and never repeats for a resource — expired and released claims still count as predecessors. sturn claim prints it; a holder passes it to sturn check <uri> --fence <n> to ask "has anything outranked me?" — the stale-usurper probe. It's advisory like the claim: second-granularity ts plus concatenation order means same-second cross-ws races resolve deterministically, not temporally.

Asks ​

Claims are pending ownership; asks are pending answers. An ask opens under its event id and stays open until an answer or cancel op lands — or ttl_secs runs out (0 = never). The question and its answer can live on different workspaces' WALs; responders filter target (actor:<n>, human:<n>, node:<h>, claim-holder:<glob>, any) to find asks meant for them.
